WHAT AN ALPHA FACTOR IS.
An alpha factor assigns a numeric score to every stock on every day (high
score -> expected out-performer / buy, low score -> expected under-performer
/ sell), and the scores are compared cross-sectionally -- across all stocks
on the same day -- to rank them. The score is computed purely from daily
price-volume data: $open $high $low $close $volume $return, transformed over
time (change, regression, rolling statistics, dispersion, correlation,
smoothing) and normalised or ranked across stocks. Its quality is how well
today's score ranks tomorrow's cross-sectional return.

WHAT AN OPTIMAL HYPOTHESIS LOOKS LIKE.
An optimal hypothesis is a SPECIFIC, measurable conditional pattern of the
form "when X holds, Y follows in next-period returns" -- a mechanism, not a
theme. It is:
  - OBSERVABLE -- fully derivable from $open/$high/$low/$close/$volume/
    $return alone, so the pattern can actually be measured from the data on
    hand. Anything needing fundamentals, news, or institutional flow is out.
  - CONDITIONAL -- it ties a measurable market state to a directional move in
    returns, with a clear sign (out-performance or under-performance).
  - GROUNDED -- it has an economic or behavioural driver (momentum, reversal,
    microstructure, liquidity, volatility regime, dispersion) that explains
    why the edge persists under noise rather than being a fluke.
  - ORIGINAL -- structurally fresh relative to over-mined textbook patterns,
    so it adds genuine search value.
Make ALL of these explicit in your reasoning:
  - KNOWLEDGE     = the conditional market pattern (when X -> Y on returns).
  - OBSERVATION   = the data pattern or prior-round result that motivates it.
  - JUSTIFICATION = the economic / behavioural driver and why it persists.
  - SPECIFICATION = which $columns carry the signal (at least one; OHLCV
